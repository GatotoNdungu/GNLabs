    Privacy

    What I collect, and what I do with it

    GN Labs is a one-person engineering practice, not a data company. This page says, plainly, what information this site gathers, why, and how to make me delete it if you'd rather I didn't have it.

    The short version: filling out a form on this site sends me your name, email, and message so I can reply. Nothing is sold, nothing is shared with advertisers, and there's no tracking beyond basic, anonymous visit counts. Everything below is the long version of that sentence.

    01

    Overview

    This site (gnlabs.site) is run by GN Labs, an independent engineering practice. When you browse, subscribe, or send a message here, some information passes through my hands. This policy explains what that is, why I need it, and what say you have over it.

    I wrote it the way I'd explain it to a client over a call, not the way a legal template would. If anything here is unclear, that's a flaw in the writing, not a trap — ask and I'll fix it.

    02

    What gets collected, and from where

    Nothing is collected passively that you didn't hand over yourself. Here's the full list, by where it comes from.

    The contact form

    Your name, email address, the system or scope you describe, and your message. Used to reply to you — nothing more.

    The newsletter form

    Just your email address, so I can send new articles roughly once a month. No name required, no profile built around it.

    Ordinary site visits

    Basic, aggregated traffic figures — which pages get read, roughly how many visits, and which country they're from. This comes from the hosting provider's server logs and from Google Analytics (GA4), set up to report in aggregate with advertising features switched off. It isn't used to identify you personally.

    What I don't collect

    No advertising cookies, no cross-site tracking, no fingerprinting, no data brokers, no selling anything to anyone. If a future feature would change that, this page gets updated first — see Changes to this policy.

    03

    How it's used

    • To reply to a message you've sent, and to keep enough context to follow up on it sensibly.
    • To send the newsletter, if you've asked for it, and to know it reached an inbox that still wants it.
    • To understand, in aggregate, which articles and pages are actually useful, so the site improves over time.
    • To meet basic legal or accounting obligations, on the rare occasion an engagement requires it.

    That's the complete list. Nothing here is used to build an advertising profile, and nothing is repurposed for a use you weren't told about at the point you handed it over.

    04

    Cookies & local storage

    This site avoids cookies wherever the browser lets it. A few small exceptions:

    • A short-lived preference, like whether you've already dismissed the newsletter prompt, stored only in your own browser and never sent anywhere.
    • Google Analytics sets a small number of first-party cookies to count visits and see which pages are read. Advertising and Google signals are turned off, and nothing is sent to Google if your browser sends a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal. You can also opt out with Google's browser add-on.
    • Standard server logs kept by the hosting provider for security and uptime, which age out automatically after a short window.

    The only cookies set are those analytics cookies; no advertising or cross-site tracking cookies are set. If that ever changes, this page gets updated first.

    05

    Third parties involved

    A handful of well-known services keep this site running. Each one only ever sees the minimum it needs to do its job:

    • Hosting infrastructure — serves the pages you're reading and keeps basic access logs.
    • Contentful — stores and serves the article text and images published on the blog. It doesn't receive anything about you as a visitor.
    • Email delivery — carries the newsletter and contact-form replies from me to you.

    None of them are permitted to use your information for their own marketing, and none of it is sold or rented to anyone, ever.

    06

    How long it's kept

    Contact-form messages are kept as long as they're useful for the conversation they came from, and then cleared out — typically within a couple of years for anything that didn't turn into ongoing work. Newsletter addresses stay on the list until you unsubscribe, which takes one click. Server logs age out automatically, usually within 30 days.

    If you'd like something removed sooner than that, see Your rights below.

    07

    Your rights over your own data

    Wherever you're writing in from, you can ask for any of the following, and I'll act on it directly rather than routing you through a form:

    See what's held

    A copy of whatever information is on file under your name or email.

    Correct it

    If something's wrong or out of date, it gets fixed on request.

    Delete it

    Ask, and it's removed — from messages to newsletter subscriptions.

    Unsubscribe

    Every newsletter email has a one-click link at the bottom. No login, no survey.

    Send any of these requests to the email below, and expect a response within a few business days — usually sooner.

    08

    Security

    The site is served over HTTPS, so anything you submit is encrypted in transit. Access to stored messages and subscriber lists is limited to me. No system is unbreakable, and if something ever did go wrong, you'd hear about it directly and promptly rather than finding out secondhand.

    09

    Children's privacy

    This site is aimed at engineers, plant operators, and technical decision-makers — it isn't built for or directed at children, and I don't knowingly collect information from anyone under 16. If you believe a child has submitted information here, get in touch and it will be removed.

    10

    Changes to this policy

    If what's collected or how it's used ever changes materially, this page will be updated and the date at the top will move. For anything significant — a new third-party tool, say, or a new use for existing data — I'll flag it in the newsletter as well, so it doesn't just sit quietly in a page nobody rereads.
