The Practice

Built for the boundaries
where specialists hand off.

Most technical problems worth paying for sit at an interface — between hardware and software, between a legacy system and its replacement, between the team that built something and the team that has to run it. GN Labs is structured to work those interfaces rather than either side of them.

01 — Range

The working range

Two halves of a single skill set. Plenty of engineers are strong on one side; the value here is in holding both at once, so the interface between them gets designed rather than negotiated after the fact.

Software side

Backends, data, and cloud

Services, APIs, pipelines, and the operational tooling around them — version control, testing, CI, observability, and the deployment discipline the operational side often never received.

Operational side

Hardware, control, plant floor

Firmware and control logic that must be deterministic, survive power cycles, and behave predictably when a sensor starts lying — with no meaningful ability to hotfix in production.

02 — Capability

Capability in detail

Six areas of practice. Engagements typically draw on three or four at once, which is rather the point.

Six areas

01

Backend & API Engineering

Services designed around the failure modes of the systems they talk to: idempotent writes, sane retry semantics, and contracts that survive the other side changing without notice.

  • Python · FastAPI · Flask
  • REST · MQTT · WebSockets
  • PostgreSQL · time-series stores
02

Data Pipelines & Observability

Moving operational data from where it is generated to where decisions get made — with buffering for the outages, and instrumentation that surfaces a gap before a customer finds it.

  • ETL & stream processing
  • InfluxDB · Grafana
  • Alerting & anomaly detection
03

Cloud & Deployment

Containerised deployments, reproducible environments, and CI that genuinely gates releases. Infrastructure sized to what is actually run, not to a reference architecture from a vendor conference.

  • Docker · CI/CD pipelines
  • Linux · nginx · TLS
  • Secrets & access boundaries
04

Embedded & Edge Systems

Firmware for devices that run unattended for months: power budgets, store-and-forward buffering, over-the-air update paths, and recovery behaviour that assumes the network is absent.

  • ESP32 · ARM Cortex-M
  • C/C++ · MicroPython
  • LoRaWAN · BLE · serial
05

Industrial Automation

PLC programs written to be read by the next engineer, not merely executed by the controller — structured text over sprawling ladder, documented state machines, staged commissioning with real rollback paths.

  • IEC 61131-3 · CODESYS
  • Modbus · PROFIBUS · Profinet
  • HMI & SCADA integration
06

Systems & Control Design

The mathematics underneath the implementation: plant modelling, loop tuning against that model, and hardware-in-the-loop validation before anything is trusted with live equipment.

  • PID & feed-forward design
  • MATLAB · Simulink
  • Hardware-in-the-loop benches

03 — Engagement

Ways to engage

Three formats. Most relationships begin with the first and progress only where it makes sense on both sides.

Format 01

Technical Diagnostic

Typically 1–2 weeks

A bounded review of an existing system — architecture, control logic, integration layer, or all three. The output is a written assessment of what is actually wrong, ranked by consequence, regardless of who performs the remediation.

  • Written findings and risk ranking
  • Remediation options with effort estimates
  • No obligation to proceed

For when something is failing and nobody agrees why.

Most common

Format 02

Fixed-Scope Build

Typically 6–16 weeks

A defined system delivered end to end: specification, implementation, commissioning, and handover documentation. Scope is agreed in writing before work begins, and changes to it are priced openly rather than absorbed quietly.

  • Staged delivery with review gates
  • Full source, drawings, and runbook
  • Defect-correction window post-commissioning

For when the outcome is clear and the path is not.

Format 03

Ongoing Retainer

Monthly, rolling

Reserved capacity each month for teams carrying systems this practice has built or reviewed. Covers iteration, incident support, and the architectural questions that are cheaper to ask early than to correct later.

  • Agreed response window
  • Priority over new enquiries
  • Offered after a first engagement

For when a system is live and still evolving.

04 — Standards

What ships with every engagement

These are not upsells. They are the definition of finished.

01

Source you own outright

Commented, version-controlled, transferred to you. No vendor lock, no encrypted blocks that cannot be opened.

02

Documentation for a stranger

Written for an engineer who has never met this practice, because eventually someone else maintains the system.

03

A tested rollback path

Every deployment stage has a documented way back that has actually been exercised, not merely assumed.

04

Handover with your team

A working session with the people who will operate it — not a PDF dropped over the wall.

05 — Fit

Where this practice adds value

Capacity is held to a small number of concurrent engagements, so it is worth being direct in both directions.

Well suited to

  • Systems that cross the hardware/software boundary and keep failing at the seam.
  • Teams with strong software practice but no in-house controls depth — or the reverse.
  • Inherited systems with no documentation and nobody left who built them.
  • Integrations that technically work but quietly lose data under load or outage.
  • Organisations that intend to own and maintain what gets built.

Not the right call for

  • Staff augmentation — a seat to fill on an existing team's backlog.
  • Large multi-team programmes needing a delivery organisation behind them.
  • Work where the technical decision is settled and only execution is wanted.
  • Engagements that cannot accommodate a modelling and validation stage.
  • Pure front-end or marketing site builds with no systems component.

06 — Questions

Practical questions

The commercial detail, stated up front rather than discovered later.

How does pricing work?

Diagnostics and fixed-scope builds are quoted as a fixed fee against a written scope, so clients are not exposed to hourly drift. Retainers are a monthly figure for reserved capacity. Quotes follow a scoping conversation — there is no rate card, because the same nominal task varies enormously depending on what it is attached to.

Is the work done on site?

Remote by default, on site where the work genuinely requires it — commissioning, panel surveys, and initial system walks usually do. Travel is agreed in advance and billed at cost.

Who owns the intellectual property?

The client does, on delivery. Generic tooling and libraries developed across engagements remain with the practice, licensed to the client perpetually — a distinction written into the agreement rather than left to assumption.

Can this run alongside an existing team or integrator?

Frequently, yes. A common arrangement is owning the integration layer while the in-house team or incumbent integrator retains their side. Boundaries of responsibility are agreed up front, because ambiguity there is usually what produced the problem in the first place.

What happens after handover?

Every build includes a defect-correction window after commissioning. Beyond that, some clients move to a retainer and others take the system fully in house — which is the intended outcome, and why the documentation standard is set where it is.
