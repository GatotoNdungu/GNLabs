    Sensor nodes & IoT firmware

    Devices you can leave in the field and forget about

    A sensor is only useful if its readings arrive. We design the whole node: the board, the power plan, the enclosure and the firmware, with the working assumption that connectivity will drop and batteries will age.

    • Power budgets worked out before a board is ordered
    • Readings stored on the device and sent when a link returns
    • Secure over-the-air updates, with a safe fallback if one fails
    • Calibration and self-check routines that flag drifting sensors

    How we work

    From first call to handover

    The same four stages on every project, sized to fit the job.

    1

    Understand

    We look at what exists, what's failing and what a good result looks like, then agree the scope in writing.

    2

    Design & prove

    Hardware and logic are designed, simulated and bench-tested before they go anywhere near live equipment.

    3

    Install & commission

    Rollout happens in stages, each with a tested way back, working around your production schedule.

    You receive source, drawings and a runbook, plus a working session with the people who will run it.

    FAQ

    Practical questions

    The commercial detail, stated up front.

    How is work priced?

    Most projects are quoted as a fixed fee against a written scope, so the price doesn't drift with the hours. Ongoing support is a monthly figure. Quotes follow a short scoping call, because the same task can vary a lot depending on what it connects to.

    Do you work on site?

    Design and software are done remotely. Site visits are used for surveys, commissioning and anything that needs hands on the equipment. Travel is agreed beforehand and billed at cost.

    Who owns the code and drawings?

    You do, on delivery and payment. Generic tools developed across projects stay with GN Labs and are licensed to you for as long as you run the system. This is spelled out in the agreement.

    Can you work alongside our existing team or contractor?

    Yes. A common setup is GN Labs owning one clearly defined part, such as the firmware or the data link, while your team keeps the rest. Responsibilities are written down before work starts.

    What happens after handover?

    Every project includes a support window after commissioning to fix anything that surfaces. After that you can run the system yourselves or keep us on a support plan. Either is fine; the documentation is written so that you can do both.
