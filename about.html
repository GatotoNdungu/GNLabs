    How we got our habits

    Lessons learned on the bench

    Every practice develops rules from the things that went wrong. These are ours, and they shape every project.

    1

    A device that can't be measured can't be trusted

    Early prototypes taught us to power up and measure every circuit on the bench before a line of production firmware is written. Problems are cheapest where nothing is installed yet.

    2

    Networks fail, so devices must cope

    Sensors that only worked while connected lost data when it mattered most. Everything we build now keeps its readings and sends them when the link returns.

    3

    Quiet failures cost more than loud ones

    A fault nobody hears about is a fault that grows. We design alerts to reach a person's phone, with escalation if nobody responds.

    4

    Documentation is part of the product

    Reconstructing drawings from memory at handover never works. Schematics, pin maps and calibration values are recorded as we go, then handed over with the source.

    5

    The goal is that you don't need us

    The best outcome is a team that runs the system confidently on its own. Everything else we do is arranged around making that possible.

    What we believe

    Four commitments behind every project

    Not slogans. These are the checks we apply before calling anything finished.

    Test first

    Hardware is powered and measured on the bench, and control logic is proven against a model, before it touches your site.

    Write it down

    Decisions, wiring and settings are recorded while they're fresh, so nothing lives only in someone's head.

    Fail loudly

    When something goes wrong, the right person finds out quickly, and the system degrades to a safe state.

    You own it

    Source code, drawings and credentials are yours, with no lock-in and nothing encrypted that you can't open.
