    01

    Overview

    By visiting gnlabs.site, reading the blog, or submitting one of the forms, you're agreeing to the terms below. They're scoped narrowly, on purpose — this is a personal engineering practice's website, not a platform, and the rules reflect that.

    If you're here because you want actual engineering work done — a retrofit, a review, a build — those terms live in a separate proposal or contract that we'd agree on together before anything starts. This page is just about the site.

    02

    Using this site

    You're welcome to browse, read, and share links to anything here. A few things aren't allowed, in the ordinary sense that most technical sites don't allow them:

    • Scraping the site at scale, or pulling the blog's content wholesale to republish or retrain something on.
    • Submitting the contact or newsletter forms with anything other than genuine information — no spam, no automated submissions.
    • Trying to probe, disrupt, or gain unauthorized access to the site or the services behind it.
    • Presenting anything on this site as your own work, or using it to imply an endorsement or partnership that doesn't exist.

    Normal reading, quoting a paragraph with a link back, and sharing an article with a colleague are all exactly what this site is for.

    03

    Where the actual engineering work is covered

    Nothing on this site — a project write-up, a blog post, a reply to a contact-form message — constitutes an engineering proposal, a quote, or a binding commitment to do work. Real engagements (a PLC retrofit, a control-loop review, a firmware build) are governed by a separate written agreement that sets out scope, deliverables, timeline, and cost, agreed on before work begins.

    If a conversation that started here turns into a project, that agreement — not this page — is what governs it.

    04

    Content and ownership

    The writing, project descriptions, diagrams, and code snippets published on this site are mine, unless a post says otherwise. You're free to reference and link to them; wholesale republishing needs a quick note first — usually a yes, but I like knowing where things end up.

    Anything you send through the contact form stays yours. Sending it to me gives me permission to read it, reply to it, and — where relevant to a project — act on it. It doesn't transfer ownership of anything you describe.

    05

    Accuracy of what's published

    The project results, figures, and technical write-ups here reflect real work, described honestly and to the best of my knowledge at the time of writing. That said, none of it should be read as a specification you can build against directly — protocols, part numbers, and standards referenced in older posts can and do get superseded.

    If you're relying on something technical you read here for your own system, verify it against current documentation, or ask — that's faster and safer than assuming a two-year-old blog post is still current.

    Links to other sites

    Some posts link out to vendor documentation, standards bodies, or tools referenced in a build. Those sites are run independently, and I'm not responsible for their content, their availability, or what they do with your data once you've clicked through. Worth reading their own terms if that matters to you.
